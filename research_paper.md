Two Winless Seasons, Two Different Failure Profiles
A focused comparison of the 2008 Detroit Lions and 2017 Cleveland Browns

Abstract
The 2008 Detroit Lions and 2017 Cleveland Browns both finished 0–16. Their aggregate performance was not identical. Detroit had a −249 point differential and allowed 517 points, while Cleveland had a −176 differential and allowed 410. Cleveland committed 41 turnovers to Detroit’s 29, gained 650 more offensive yards, but scored 34 fewer points. The data support a multidimensional conclusion: Detroit was worse by scoring margin and defensive outcomes, while Cleveland was worse in turnovers and drive-scoring efficiency.

Research question
Which of these two 0–16 teams performed worse, and what dimensions explain the difference?

Scope and method
The only comparison subjects are Detroit’s 2008 season and Cleveland’s 2017 season. The project uses season-level team aggregates, situational summaries, full regular-season game logs for both teams, and a selected player-contribution panel from Pro-Football-Reference. Metrics are compared directly; no causal claims are made.

Results
Detroit scored 268 points and allowed 517 (−249); Cleveland scored 234 and allowed 410 (−176). Cleveland’s point differential was 73 points better. Detroit allowed 32.31 points per game and 6.4 opponent yards per play, compared with Cleveland’s 25.63 points and 5.1 yards per play. Detroit’s defensive profile was therefore worse in both scoring and yards-per-play terms.

Cleveland’s offense produced 4,942 yards, 650 more than Detroit’s 4,292, and 4.9 yards per play compared with Detroit’s 4.7. However, Cleveland scored 34 fewer points and had a 22.0% drive-scoring rate compared with Detroit’s 26.2%. Cleveland turned the ball over 41 times, including 28 interceptions, while Detroit had 29 turnovers and 19 interceptions. Cleveland also had more penalties (110 vs 88) and penalty yards (924 vs 729).

Situationally, Cleveland converted third downs at 33.8% (73/216), compared with Detroit’s 28.8% (59/205). Detroit had a higher red-zone touchdown rate (19/31, 61.3%) than Cleveland (19/39, 48.7%). The split is consistent with Cleveland moving the ball in some ways but failing to convert enough possessions into points while giving away the ball more often.

Player contribution context
Detroit’s passing was distributed across four primary quarterbacks: Dan Orlovsky (1,616 yards, 8 TD, 8 INT), Daunte Culpepper (786 yards, 4 TD, 6 INT), Jon Kitna (758 yards, 5 TD, 5 INT), and Drew Stanton (119 yards, 1 TD, 0 INT). Kevin Smith rushed for 976 yards and 8 TD; Calvin Johnson recorded 1,331 receiving yards and 12 TD. Cleveland relied heavily on DeShone Kizer (2,894 passing yards, 11 TD, 22 INT), with Kevin Hogan (517 yards, 4 TD, 5 INT) and Cody Kessler (126 yards, 0 TD, 1 INT) also appearing. Isaiah Crowell rushed for 853 yards; Duke Johnson had 693 receiving yards. These season lines are descriptive and do not isolate player-level causal value.

Verdict
If “worst” means the greatest season-wide scoring deficit and defensive collapse, the 2008 Lions were worse: −249 point differential, 517 points allowed, and 6.4 opponent yards per play. If it means offensive ball security and converting production into points, the 2017 Browns have the stronger case: 41 turnovers, 28 interceptions, fewer points scored, and a lower drive-scoring rate despite more yards. The overall answer depends on the weighting of these dimensions; a single unqualified ranking would conceal meaningful differences.

Limitations
The environment could not download nflverse’s raw 2008 and 2017 play-by-play files because the host could not resolve GitHub. Consequently EPA per play, success rate, explosive-play frequency, play-level field-position adjustments, full drive-level tables, and play-by-play special-teams analysis are not computed here. The player table is a key-contributor panel, not every player’s complete statistics. Aggregate source figures are rounded where indicated. No synthetic plays or model outputs were invented.

Next analytical layer
The included scripts are designed to acquire only the 2008 and 2017 play-by-play files and calculate target-team EPA/play, success rate, play-type totals, and target-team game metadata once those source files are available. Those outputs must be validated before being incorporated into conclusions.
